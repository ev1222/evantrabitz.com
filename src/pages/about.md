## Education

I grew up in Columbus, Ohio before attending Washington University in St. Louis. Despite starting out in mechanical engineering, and after a few weeks psychology as well, I eventually settled on computer science, philosophy, and physics, graduating with honors in 2023.  
## Career
My career thus far has been marked by autonomy: twice now, my mentors have entrusted me to take on their work, and I've risen to the challenge faster than I thought possible. Every team I've been on has been small and self-directed, operating like a startup within a larger organization. Most recently, I have chosen this autonomy for myself, and taken on even more ambitious projects with it.
### ICA
After a bit of an unplanned post-graduation break, my career started in January 2024 at International Consulting Associates ([ICA](https://www.ica.ai/)), a federal health contractor. One of our primary contracts was to develop an application for medical device reviewers. Everything from tongue depressors to heart valves had to go through these reviewers, and at the time (~2024) there was no standard form for companies to submit, so the proposals could be a 2 page summary or 1000 page lab report. Reviewers ensured the proposals met regulator standards and hunted for evidence of fraud. Our application leveraged ML pipelines to streamline their reviews. 

I started out completely green but quickly learned from my colleagues, developing prototypes like an image caption generator with vision-language models or NLQ system with deterministic SQL-parsing guardrails. Summer 2024 I took on full-stack development by rebuilding parts of the application with more modern frameworks, picking up a deep understanding of synthetic data along the way. 

Eventually, by September 2024, I had started to lead the company's ML Engineering. I came into this role by drafting up alternative solutions to our document processing problem and eventually was given a shot to show what I could do. Also, at this point, most of my colleagues who mentored me had moved on, leaving me to develop solo. As such, I developed an overhauled version of the pipeline in AWS in seven weeks and had it delivering data straight to the frontend. 

I came out of it a *much* stronger developer. I learned more about document processing methods than I think I ever would have guessed possible; more formal methods of system design; how to go from a spec to a product; how to juggle multiple stakeholder demands on a deadline; proper data science techniques and terminology; and, most importantly, how to actually ship and deliver. 

By Summer 2025, two things were nagging at me to leave. 
1. Externally, the changing political picture meant what was once stable government work became a lot more unpredictable, and
2. Internally, I realized I had reached the limits of my growth.
I started putting out feelers for a new role which is when one of my ex-colleagues reached out with an opening at TRSS. 
### TRSS 
Thomson Reuters Special Services ([TRSS](https://www.trssllc.com/)) is a U.S.-based subsidiary of Thomson Reuters specializing in national security. We worked across many different agencies on projects I can't discuss publicly; however, I was hired on as a data scientist for one of the few projects that is public: [Spotlight by Canary ](https://www.spotlight.ngo/), a tool used by NGOs and agencies across the country for child sex trafficking investigations. Heavy work, but a great mission that drove everyone working on it — myself included. 

Less than three months into the job I took over as lead data scientist on the project. Not quite what I was expecting when I took the job (I figured I'd be entering a more structured corporate environment), but I relished the opportunity to learn more, take on more responsibility, and lead research tasks, of which there was no shortage.

Most of my work on Spotlight revolved around identity resolution under adversarial conditions. I dealt with large scales of data, on the scale of billions of records. Working within a nonprofit budget meant I had to get creative with the solutions I developed: open-source models, compute-constrained training, extrapolating from small-scale experiments, and so on.  In addition to my work on Spotlight I participated on other projects at TRSS; each employee is encouraged to take 10% time that they spend on innovation, and I used mine to develop things like information ontologies and supply chain intelligence.

What stood out to me most during my time at TRSS was how much of a difference having a mission associated with my work made for me. I spent *many* late nights on Spotlight, but it never bothered me because I believed so deeply in what we were accomplishing together and received constant feedback on the success of that mission, like weekly reports of children the platform successfully identified. 

So why leave? I wasn't actively looking for alternate roles, but got contacted by a recruiter. They were offering a role doing greenfield development of AI workflows and models for a financial enterprise; in other words, total control building cutting-edge solutions to real business problems on a small team with virtually unlimited resources. After giving it much thought, and even talking it over with TRSS, I decided that such an opportunity at this point in my career was too big to pass up on.  
### BWE
Wanting more freedom in what I built, I moved to Bellwether Enterprise (BWE) to build greenfield AI tools and lead the company's AI governance and evaluations.
